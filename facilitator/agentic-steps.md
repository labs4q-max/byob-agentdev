# Marquise's Walkthrough — A First Taste of AI Systems

**Audience:** Nontechnical business owners.  
**Suggested time:** 15 minutes, with extra time for questions.  
**Takeaway:** A reusable instruction set, business notes, and reviewed sample replies.

This is the beginner version of the Agents & Systems portion. Live inbox connections, website agents, booking, research automation, and dashboards are future topics. The hands-on win today is one useful chat workflow.

## Opening — 1 minute

"You've already worked on your brand. Now we're going to give an assistant a job, show it how your business sounds, and tell it what a good result looks like. By the end, you'll have something you can copy, change, and use again."

Explain that this is a chat-based demonstration of agentic steps. The assistant processes text the attendee supplies. It does not monitor an inbox, run on its own, or take outside actions.

## Brief the assistant — 2 minutes

Use the same recipe throughout:

| Part | What it means | Inbox example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Role | Give it one clear job. | Read and draft replies to customer emails. |
| Personality | Tell it how the brand sounds. | Warm, clear, helpful, and brief. |
| Execution | Define the result and its limits. | Prioritize, draft, check facts, and flag owner decisions. |

The business notes are the reference sheet. Tell attendees: "Your assistant needs the current facts. It cannot know a policy you haven't given it."

## Run it — 5 minutes

Open [Start Here](../START-HERE.md). Have everyone copy its complete block into a new AI chat.

Use the fictional business first so everyone can compare results. A phone is fine; no downloads or coding are required.

### The agentic steps

| Step | What happens in the exercise | Who handles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read | Identify each customer's request and the relevant business facts. | Assistant |
| Prioritize | Assign High, Medium, or Low using the supplied rules. | Assistant |
| Draft | Prepare short replies with the brand's voice. | Assistant |
| Check | Compare drafts with the notes and flag gaps or approvals. | Assistant |
| Review | Verify facts, correct wording, and resolve owner decisions. | Attendee |
| Keep and reuse | Copy the results and save the instructions for later. | Attendee |

If the human review finds an error, ask the assistant to revise and review again. Self-checking is useful but does not guarantee accuracy.

## Compare and improve — 4 minutes

Ask attendees to check **tone, facts, and next step**.

Expected order:
1. Jordan: High — event tomorrow; order lookup and refund review required.
2. Morgan: Medium — custom order needs a quote and feasibility review.
3. Taylor: Low — shipping, cost, and hours are already in the notes.

Use [example responses](../responses/example-responses.md) as a reference, not an exact script. Different wording can still meet the standard.

Point out two important catches:
- Ten business days since purchase does not establish when an order dispatched.
- A ready-made T-shirt price does not establish a custom order quote.

Have attendees ask for one revision, such as a warmer tone, while preserving facts and approval limits.

## Take it home — 2 minutes

Show the [responses folder](../responses/README.md). Attendees can copy the sample or blank worksheet even if downloads fail.

Their optional folder structure is simple:

| Folder | Keep here |
| --- | --- |
| brand | Current business facts and voice |
| agents | Reusable role, personality, and execution instructions |
| responses | Their privately saved drafts and edits |

These folders organize files; they do not connect themselves to a chat. Paste the current notes and prompt when starting a new conversation.

Keep today's activity focused on fictional messages. Attendees can personalize the brand notes and use made-up questions to practice at home.

## Close

"Today you gave an assistant a job, a voice, and a standard. You can take this home and repeat it with your own business notes. A future session can cover connecting a real inbox, controlling what the assistant is allowed to do, and reviewing its work."

Do not promise that a future session is already scheduled. Do not describe today's draft exercise as a business that now runs itself.
